# The world inside a breath

A breath gives an immense world somewhere intimate to begin. Attend to its beginning and end, then imagine an entire life passing within it. From inside that life there were mornings, work, waiting and people who mattered. From the other frame, barely a moment. What would let a reader encounter both scales without losing the person inside the smaller one?

This is one of the questions behind Sleepwalker. In [Breath of Cosmos](https://getharsh.in/blog/breath-of-cosmos/) (on getHarsh), I begin with a breathing pattern as an input to an imagined model of consciousness, then extend the comparison into cosmic time. The argument makes a definite choice: the cosmic duration is unbounded, while the human breath remains a finite, nonzero interval. Its ratio is infinite. Here I want to follow what that construction makes possible for a story and an image.

<a id="the-interval-stays-small-the-frame-keeps-opening"></a>

## The interval stays small; the frame keeps opening

Take the essay's illustrative human breath of seven seconds. Let $B$ be the cosmic duration, expressed in seconds. Then the two directions of the comparison are

$$
\lim_{B\to\infty}\frac{B}{7}=\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{B\to\infty}\frac{7}{B}=0.
$$

The human interval has not become zero. Its proportion tends to zero as the other duration grows without bound. This matters to the imaginative movement: a life does not need to disappear for the frame containing it to become immeasurably larger.

The essay uses breath and time as proxies in a thought experiment about consciousness. Its further possibility is a cosmic or collective consciousness containing individual forms of experience. The limit expresses the scale relation; the interpretation asks what it might mean to belong to that whole. A story gives that question a witness. The witness has a particular history, even when the world exceeds the history's familiar terms.

<a id="a-calendar-can-lead-the-imagination-towards-the-opening"></a>

## A calendar can lead the imagination towards the opening

Vedic cosmological accounts give the reader several changes of scale to encounter. In the Bhaktivedanta edition of the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*, [3.11.18](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/11/18/) gives a four-yuga cycle whose commentary expresses it as 4.32 million human years. [3.11.22](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/11/22/) places a thousand such cycles within Brahmā's day, followed by a night of equal duration. [3.11.38](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/11/38/) then compares the two halves of Brahmā's life with a moment for the Supreme. A lifetime on one scale becomes a brief interval on another.

Each specified period is finite, however immense it feels beside a human life. The [*Brahma-saṁhitā*, 5.48](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bs/5/48/), supplies the image of the Brahmās' lives within Mahā-Vishnu's breath. *Breath of Cosmos* takes that image further: suppose there is no final bound to the duration we are comparing with our own.

The notes calculate how many seven-second intervals fit within one of those finite periods. Here I want to stay with the next step. What happens to the world we thought we knew when the frame around it can keep opening?

<a id="the-place-the-witness-knows"></a>

## The place the witness knows

In [Markandeya's encounter](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/vedic-world-inquiry/through-markandeyas-encounters/markandeya-and-the-size-of-a-world/), the impossible scale arrives through an ordinary recognition. The witness finds a world inside the child's body, then recognises his own hermitage. The familiar remains there after the frame has become impossible. That recognition makes the encounter more unsettling than a succession of larger containers would have been.

I can imagine drawing the change as a series of receding views: a person, a landscape, a world, another world around it. The figure becomes a marker of size. But what if I keep the reader close to the figure? A remembered path could remain recognisable while its surroundings cease to fit the account. A detail that seemed reassuring could become the very thing that makes the new world impossible to dismiss.

The two compositions offer different experiences of the same ambition. One tells the reader how large the world is. The other lets them discover that the world they know has acquired a place within something they cannot yet understand. The second gives the person more to do than demonstrate scale.

<a id="what-continues-between-the-frames"></a>

## What continues between the frames?

A graphic novel can make that discovery happen through sequence. A place first encountered as ordinary can return with a changed relation to the world around it. The reader carries the earlier encounter across the interval between panels. If every detail changes, recognition becomes difficult; if nothing changes, the expansion may never become an encounter at all.

That brings the cosmic question into the craft of [keeping something recognisable through a sequence](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/from-frame-to-sequence/continuity-is-more-than-a-seed/). Which feature must survive? Where should the reader first notice the difference? What can the witness recognise before they can explain it? These are decisions an author can work on without asking the picture to prove a theory of consciousness.

A small experience can be contained within a larger account and still be the experience through which that account becomes available. The breath supplies a rhythm. The construction opens the scale. The witness brings a world into the encounter, and the story has to let us feel what happens when that world is no longer the whole.

## A finite period beside the unbounded construction

A finite comparison provides a useful step towards the essay's change of scale. It combines a textual period, a calendar convention and a chosen interval. The 4.32 million human years comes from the commentary accompanying [Bhāgavata Purāṇa 3.11.18](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/11/18/); the 365-day year and seven-second breath are the illustration's choices. The arithmetic can be examined by changing those choices separately.

$$
N=\frac{4{,}320{,}000\times365\times24\times60\times60}{7}\approx1.9462\times10^{13}.
$$

That is roughly nineteen trillion intervals.

**Note**

**This particular count is finite.** The essay's infinite ratio instead uses an unbounded numerator and the same positive seven-second denominator.

<a id="gazette-notes-two-different-changes-to-a-ratio"></a>

### Two different changes to a ratio

Let $T$ be the long duration and $t$ the chosen short interval. The count is $N=T/t$. Describing both durations in minutes instead of seconds divides both by sixty and leaves $N$ unchanged. Choosing a fourteen-second interval instead of a seven-second one halves $N$. The first operation changes the unit in which the same quantities are written; the second changes the interval being counted. A larger number can therefore come from a smaller counting interval without any enlargement of the long period.

The dependence is exact:

$$
\frac{T/k}{t/k}=\frac{T}{t}=N,
\qquad \frac{T}{kt}=\frac Nk\quad(k>0).
$$

Changing the illustrative year from 365 to 366 days, while keeping the number of years and the seven-second interval fixed, multiplies the count by $366/365$, an increase of about 0.274 percent. This comparison does not decide which calendar a textual scheme requires. It shows that a convention about the long duration and a choice about the short interval enter the count in different places. Neither is a property of a witness's awareness.

<a id="gazette-notes-a-count-and-a-limit"></a>

### A count and a limit

For finite positive $T$ and $t$, $N$ is finite. The family $T_m=mT$, with fixed $t$ and integer $m$ growing without bound, instead gives $N_m=mN$, which grows without bound. That is a precise way to express the unbounded premise of *Breath of Cosmos*. No member of the family needs to be infinite for its counts to grow without bound. The four-yuga period remains a finite textual quantity; it can supply a starting scale without supplying an upper bound for the thought experiment.

The limit establishes the scale relationship. The essay then asks what it would mean for individual experience to belong to a cosmic or collective consciousness. That interpretation goes beyond counting intervals. If it were developed into a probabilistic model of experiences, it would also need to specify which experiences are possible and how they can occur, much as the typing-monkey comparison requires a sampling rule. Keeping that next question visible preserves the reach of the interpretation and the particular result the calculation supplies.
