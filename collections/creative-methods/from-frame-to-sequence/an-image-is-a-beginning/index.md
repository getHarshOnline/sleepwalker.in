# An image is a beginning

A generated frame can show me something I did not know how to ask for. That is part of its appeal: the image arrives before the intention is fully verbal, and choosing what to keep can become a way of discovering the intention. Carrying the discovery into another frame asks a harder question. What, exactly, was worth keeping?

<a id="the-part-that-mattered"></a>

## The part that mattered

A small figure before an enormous threshold can hold the relationship I want even when its materials are wrong. Another image can have exquisite surfaces and lose the relationship entirely. If I judge both by how impressive they look in isolation, I have no reliable way to choose between them.

Sleepwalker's visual direction begins with the human anchor. The environment carries scale, but the person gives that scale a reason to be felt. A cool field and warm focal light can guide attention towards that relation; weathered surfaces can make the environment feel like something encountered rather than freshly invented. A return path or an object someone has maintained can suggest [a world inhabited beyond its spectacle](https://getharsh.in/collections/meaning-models-and-creative-practice/a-future-that-remembers/). Those treatments work when the composition already holds the person and the threshold together. The prompt becomes a way to discover which decision should survive the next attempt, rather than a request to produce the whole work at once.

![A red-scarfed figure stands on a stone forecourt, facing a distant circular tower across water channels; the dark figure interrupts the warm central axis of the city.](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/from-frame-to-sequence/an-image-is-a-beginning/forecourt.webp)

*The closing composition from Sleepwalker's Home.*

The figure stands directly on the approach to the circular tower. Its dark silhouette interrupts the warm central line, while the scarf gives the nearby body a small movement against the enormous, nearly symmetrical setting. The city could remain impressive without the figure; with it, the image asks where someone is standing and what they are facing. That is a relation I can point to and try to preserve.

<a id="one-prompt-cannot-hold-every-decision-equally"></a>

## One prompt cannot hold every decision equally

Composition, character identity, expression, environment and lettering are different kinds of work. Asking one generation to solve them together makes it difficult to improve one without disturbing the others. The route I intend to explore begins with frames I direct in Midjourney, followed by local ComfyUI and controlled refinement that separates those decisions. A composition can remain fixed while a character's treatment changes; a pose can be specified separately from the appearance of the person holding it. Lettering can be composed for the page rather than left to chance in an image.

Separating those decisions lets each change become more legible. If a revised pose turns inquiry into confrontation, I can see what the pose did without also changing the environment. If the colour treatment moves the emotional centre, I can reconsider that treatment while retaining the staging. Controlled work makes room for discovery because fewer unrelated choices are lost with each attempt.

<a id="making-a-change-without-losing-the-frame"></a>

## Making a change without losing the frame

A workable study can start in the least polished form: a thumbnail fixing the small figure, the opening and the masses beyond it. At that scale, a stronger silhouette can do more than a more intricate building. I would carry the chosen composition into a pose or depth condition, develop the person's identity separately, and then work on the environment around the person. Masks make the separation practical: an expression can change inside one region while the important contour and light direction outside it remain deliberate choices.

Spatial conditioning is useful here because it holds a different sort of information from a text prompt. The [ControlNet paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Zhang_Adding_Conditional_Control_to_Text-to-Image_Diffusion_Models_ICCV_2023_paper.html) develops conditions including edges, depth and human pose. A depth map can help preserve the threshold's spatial relation while a pose condition gives the person a specific action. Neither names the emotional proposition of the scene. That remains in the staging: whether the shoulders yield, whether the head looks into the space, and how much distance the composition leaves before entry.

Environment, lettering and final paint-over then answer their own questions. A ruin can carry mass and silence without an indiscriminate layer of luminous glyphs. Lettering needs space before the surface detail fills the page. Paint-over can recover a hand, a face or a material boundary that the generation made plausible but narratively wrong. These are not failures to automate enough. They are places where the work needs a decision finer than “make another image”.

For the threshold study, the stages would carry different decisions forward:

| Stage | Decision worth retaining | What can still change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thumbnail | Small figure, opening and large surrounding masses | Materials, detail and expression |
| Spatial conditioning | Pose and depth relationship | Surface treatment within that geometry |
| Character and environment passes | Recognisable person and the light connecting them to the threshold | Local corrections inside selected masks |
| Lettering and paint-over | Reading order, gesture and emotional focus | Detail that competes with the sequence |

Keeping the conditioning inputs and model choices makes an attempt repeatable enough to investigate, but the thumbnail remains an important check against false progress. A beautiful render that hides the witness behind ornament has lost the composition's point. A rougher one that preserves the relation may be the better foundation. The sequence must retain the decision the frame helped me discover.

<a id="the-image-can-disagree-with-the-idea"></a>

## The image can disagree with the idea

A drawing may reveal that a scene's supposed emotional centre is somewhere else, or expose a weakness in the written premise. I want that disagreement to be possible: the image can send a question back to the work, and I can choose whether to follow it. The same openness matters in [reading a tradition](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/vedic-world-inquiry/through-markandeyas-encounters/reading-a-tradition/), where a source can resist what I hoped to make from it. When a frame does reveal a compelling relation, [keeping the character through a small sequence](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/from-frame-to-sequence/continuity-is-more-than-a-seed/) lets me follow how that relation changes. What survives is a choice I can understand and develop, rather than a look I can only try to repeat.
