# Keeping a character through a sequence

The reader sees a change before they know why it happened. In a comic, that is powerful. In generated art, it can also be an accident. If the face, clothing or position of a carried object changes between two panels, the reader may take it as an event. A production inconsistency has acquired narrative meaning before anyone intended it to.

An early extraction for Sleepwalker's website made this problem concrete. The request was to separate a standing figure from its forest background. In the source, the figure's chest faced the viewer, with the head turned slightly to one side; the first extraction showed the figure's back. A change meant to remove scenery had changed the encounter: facing the reader and turning away invite different readings of attention and intention. The corrective request specified the original facing direction, shoulder position, lowered hands, proportions, clothing and bare feet. It asked for the person to remain the same while the background disappeared. The important constraint was the relation the pose carried, not simply a recognisable face.

<a id="recognition-has-more-than-one-input"></a>

## Recognition has more than one input

Consider a neutral sequence: a person notices a doorway, approaches it and turns back. Expression and pose change. The silhouette, proportions and clothing need enough continuity for the person to remain recognisable. The doorway moves within the frame as the viewpoint changes, but its relation to the room must still make sense.

A continuity sheet describes the person across views; a pose guide describes the action; a composition sketch describes where that action sits. A seed records one generation input rather than defining any of these relationships. The [ControlNet paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Zhang_Adding_Conditional_Control_to_Text-to-Image_Diffusion_Models_ICCV_2023_paper.html) describes spatial conditioning through inputs such as edges, depth and pose, giving the process a way to hold some spatial relations more deliberately. Character recognition still depends on which features remain legible through the changed view, and narrative meaning depends on what the sequence does with them.

<a id="a-different-image-can-still-be-the-same-event"></a>

## A different image can still be the same event

Sleepwalker's visual direction allows the same event to change with viewpoint, memory or emotional state. A recovered memory can have a different material treatment from a lived event while retaining the person, room and significant gesture. Recognition carries the relation across the change; the altered treatment lets the reader experience another way of approaching it.

The stable relation gives the variation something to disturb. A repeated composition with one changed detail can focus attention on that detail. If every feature changes at once, the reader may lose the very connection through which the variation was meant to speak. Continuity therefore creates expressive range rather than requiring every frame to look identical.

<a id="the-register-changes-for-a-reason"></a>

## The register changes for a reason

The doorway study can move through several visual registers without changing its event. In a lived view, clear silhouettes and a cool illuminated field keep the person near. In recovered memory, amber and worn print can change the material distance while retaining the doorway and the gesture. A dream might begin with those same particulars and break only one relation: the opening grows beyond the wall that contains it, while the hand and the room remain recognisable. The impossible scale becomes legible because the rest of the image continues to honour the ordinary setting.

That single break is more useful than unrestricted surrealism. If the face, room, pose and viewpoint all drift, the reader cannot locate what became impossible. A maskable transformation also gives production a tractable question: preserve the agreed identity and geometry, then apply the intended break to the chosen relation. Dream logic has become a compositional operation rather than a prompt for strangeness.

For a study in corrupted attribution, I could repeat two nearly symmetric panels and alter just a shadow or the direction of a speech-balloon tail. The observation remains familiar while the reader's account of who acted becomes uncertain. The altered detail has to survive the intended viewing size; a clue that only exists at production resolution cannot do the same work on a phone. The change gives uncertainty a visible location in an otherwise familiar composition.

Resolution need not erase the difference. Two viewpoints could retain distinct palettes on one spread while their shared spatial relation finally aligns. The views become intelligible together, not interchangeable. In that possibility, continuity preserves both recognition and plurality: the same encounter can be followed through more than one way of seeing it.

| Register | Relation retained | Deliberate variation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lived event | Person, doorway and action | Clear silhouettes in a cool illuminated field |
| Recovered memory | Recognisable place and gesture | Amber, worn-print material distance |
| Dream | Hand and ordinary room | Opening grows beyond its containing wall |
| Corrupted attribution | Familiar observation | A shadow or balloon tail changes who seems responsible |
| Resolution | Shared spatial relation | Distinct viewpoints align without losing their palettes |

<a id="the-record-explains-an-attempt-not-its-meaning"></a>

## The record explains an attempt, not its meaning

Keeping the workflow, models, conditioning inputs and parameters makes a changed result easier to inspect. Judging the sequence requires another scale of attention. A consistent face may draw attention away from the action; a speech balloon may cover the gesture that connects two panels; a page legible at full size may lose the relation on a phone. The production record explains an attempt, while thumbnails, lettering and the intended viewing size expose what that attempt lets a reader see.

Recognition lets a change become meaningful: the reader sees that this is the person they knew, now turning away, returning or noticing something. [Between the panels](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/from-frame-to-sequence/the-gutter-is-an-inference/), they supply what the images leave out. Continuity gives that participation a stable world to act upon.

## A small continuity study

An invented three-panel doorway sequence offers a small study of recognisability. Varying the view and then the material treatment would separate two ways a sequence can lose the person it is meant to follow.

<a id="gazette-notes-separating-a-change-of-view-from-a-change-of-person"></a>

### Separating a change of view from a change of person

One proposed comparison holds the room layout and clothing design fixed while the person notices the doorway, approaches it and turns back. Pose and viewpoint vary. The question is whether the silhouette, proportions, clothing and placement of a carried object keep the person recognisable through those changes. Copying one face into all three views would evade part of the problem rather than solve it.

A second comparison keeps the intended action and staging but changes the material register, for example from a lived view to recovered memory. This asks whether recognition survives the treatment change. If the first comparison already fails, the second cannot tell us whether the material change caused the loss. Establishing ordinary continuity first makes the expressive variation easier to interpret.

[Zhang, Rao and Agrawala's ControlNet paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Zhang_Adding_Conditional_Control_to_Text-to-Image_Diffusion_Models_ICCV_2023_paper.html) supplies spatial-conditioning techniques, including edges, depth, segmentation and pose. A workflow record would explain which inputs produced an attempt. Recognition belongs to the resulting sequence: matching settings cannot substitute for a person remaining legible through the turn.

<a id="gazette-notes-three-scales-of-inspection"></a>

### Three scales of inspection

| Viewing scale | What can remain clear | What can disappear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thumbnail | Movement, doorway and turn | Small identity details |
| Lettered page | Gesture and speaker order | An action covered by a balloon |
| Intended reading size | The features carrying recognition or a clue | Fine distinctions visible only at production resolution |

**Tip**

**Compare the sequence at all three viewing scales.** A frame can succeed at one scale and fail at another. The point of comparing them is to find where the reading breaks, not to give every render an identical checklist score.

The comparisons separate identity, staging and treatment sufficiently to make a failed sequence informative about the next revision.
