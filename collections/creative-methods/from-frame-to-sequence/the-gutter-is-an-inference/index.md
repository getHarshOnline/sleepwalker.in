# What happens between the panels

A hand approaches a closed door. In the next panel, a person stands inside the room. The door opened somewhere between the images, although nobody drew it opening. The reader made that part of the story happen, connecting the two visible moments with an action the page left out.

Change the second panel to the person still outside, facing a locked door, and the relationship changes. Add somebody else holding a key and another explanation becomes possible. The images do not need a caption announcing each inference. Their arrangement gives the reader something to do.

![A person reaches towards a closed door; in the next panel, that person stands inside the room.](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/from-frame-to-sequence/the-gutter-is-an-inference/doorway-consequence.svg)

**A: the next frame supplies a consequence.** Outside → inside.

![A person reaches towards a closed door; in the next panel, they remain outside beside another person holding a key.](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/from-frame-to-sequence/the-gutter-is-an-inference/doorway-possibility.svg)

**B: the next frame opens another possibility.** Outside → outside, with someone holding a key.

The first frame is the same. The different second frames invite different accounts of the action left between them.

<a id="the-strongest-image-may-be-wrong-for-the-page"></a>

## The strongest image may be wrong for the page

A beautiful establishing frame can be redundant if the previous panel has already established the place. A spectacular interior can expose what a later page needs to withhold. A panel belongs to an action, a rhythm and an order of discovery: it may introduce the situation, sustain an unresolved moment, carry its turn or leave room for the consequence. A quieter frame can do more for the page if it lets the reader recognise what has changed.

<a id="the-missing-action-belongs-to-the-mystery"></a>

## The missing action belongs to the mystery

A later revelation can revise the explanation of what the reader saw without replacing the observation. The door was closed; perhaps someone else opened it. The first explanation was incomplete, not necessarily the image. That possibility depends on ordinary continuity. If a handle changes sides because generation drifted, it may look like a clue; repeated accidents make it hard to know which discrepancies deserve attention. [Keeping continuity through a sequence](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/from-frame-to-sequence/continuity-is-more-than-a-seed/) gives the reader a baseline against which an intentional discrepancy can matter.

<a id="attention-changes-the-interval"></a>

## Attention changes the interval

A sequence can sustain the doorway without repeatedly establishing it. One panel gives the place, another the hand approaching, a narrow interval holds the hesitation, and the next carries the irreversible discovery. A release panel can then show what the discovery did to the person. Changing the space given to hesitation changes how long the reader inhabits it, even when the imagined event lasts no longer. Moment-to-moment transitions attend to small changes; aspect-to-aspect views can gather a place through several details before its full relation becomes apparent.<sup><a id="fnref-note-7465726d73"></a>[1](#fn-note-7465726d73)</sup>

Words also take time to cross a page. A line can reach us before its speaker appears, carrying a voice through the interval. Where that voice belongs depends on [the lettering and the space around it](https://sleepwalker.in/collections/creative-methods/words-and-the-space-around-them/), another part of the composition rather than a caption explaining what the images already show.

<a id="the-page-has-a-body"></a>

## The page has a body

A reveal placed after a page turn arrives differently from the same reveal visible on the current spread. A phone's scroll is another physical arrangement: the words and images may be unchanged while the timing is not. I am interested in that material part of reading, the distance between frames, the density of a page, the room a silence receives. A dream sequence can alter one of those relations and let the disturbance travel through an otherwise recognisable page.

What the page leaves out can become part of what it expresses. [The event, the character's account and the reader's discovery](https://sleepwalker.in/blog/information-architecture-for-a-story/) meet in that space, with the reader supplying a relation rather than receiving its explanation. The door can stay unchanged on the page while becoming a different door in the reader's memory.
