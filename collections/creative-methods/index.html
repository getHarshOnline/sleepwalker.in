Creative methods

How visual intention, generative tools and structured continuity can support deliberate making.

A figure at a threshold can be smaller than the opening and still hold the centre of a composition. Moving the figure, widening the frame or changing the contrast alters that relationship. The useful discovery is more particular than “this looks good”: something in the arrangement makes the image carry an idea.

In Sleepwalker's Home, the closing view puts a red-scarfed figure on the approach to a circular tower. The city has scale, but the small person facing it gives me a way into that scale. An image is a beginning starts with that frame and asks what would need to survive a revision.

Generative tools make variations available quickly. The craft is in recognising the choice worth developing and carrying it into another frame. Pose and expression need to change without erasing identity; a new view needs to belong to the same space. Controlled refinement can preserve selected relationships while leaving others open to invention. The progression from frame to sequence takes that problem into a page someone else can read.

Continuity reaches beyond appearance. An event can happen before a character understands it, and a reader can see its consequence before its cause. Those different orders of a story let a revelation change the significance of an earlier panel without changing what happened there. Even the position of a few words can change whose account the reader receives. Structure makes those relationships workable; composition and pacing give them an experience.

A variation can reveal something worth keeping. Put two good images next to each other, though, and they may tell a story neither told alone. That is where making the page starts to change the idea.
