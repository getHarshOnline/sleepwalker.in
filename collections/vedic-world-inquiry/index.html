The Vedic-world inquiry

Markandeya's changing encounters and the movement of inward sound open questions about scale, attention and devotion.

What does Markandeya recognise inside a world contained by a child, and after that impossible encounter, what does he ask for? The passage turns somewhere between the two. I want to follow that turn, including the part that a retelling centred on cosmic spectacle might leave behind.

These readings begin with questions about time, consciousness and cause, then follow what the particular account says. In the Bhāgavata, one encounter with the cosmic child continues into another. In the Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā, attention follows sound towards silence. The names and images can feel familiar before their movement is. Reading the passage, and knowing which translation I am reading, gives that familiarity something to answer to.

A story or an image may grow from the encounter. Sometimes the reading simply changes the question. The essays here leave room for both.
