# Markandeya and the size of a world

The detail that holds my attention is not just the size of the universe. It is that the familiar world is still there. In the Bhaktivedanta rendering of the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*, [12.9.27](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/9/27/), Markandeya is drawn into the child's body with an inhalation. What he encounters inside is a world he recognises. The narrative turns what seemed to contain everything into something contained, then lets recognition complicate the impossible change of scale.

<a id="the-ordinary-survives-the-extraordinary"></a>

## The ordinary survives the extraordinary

The following [verses, 12.9.28–29](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/9/28-29/), do not remain at the level of abstract cosmic grandeur. The account includes rivers, settlements, occupations and the divisions of time. In [12.9.30](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/9/30/), the witness recognises his own hermitage before the child's exhalation returns him to the waters.

That movement changes the frame without making the particulars irrelevant. Rivers, work and the divisions of time give the witness more than an abstract universe to contemplate. Recognition reaches as far as his own hermitage, something intimate within the immense account. My reading is that an altered perspective can make familiar life strange without making it disposable. The encounter's force comes through the persistence of particulars in a place where the witness did not expect them.

<a id="the-witness-gives-the-scale-somewhere-to-land"></a>

## The witness gives the scale somewhere to land

A diagram can show one container inside another. A narrative gives the change to someone who has to undergo it. Recognition, bewilderment and the return to a previous frame make cosmic scale a sequence of experiences. I can follow the encounter through a witness who cannot immediately reconcile what he sees, even when I cannot reconcile the frames myself. That is part of what draws me towards Markandeya in Sleepwalker's wider inquiry: his limits matter alongside what he witnesses. A world vast enough to disorient him still contains a place he knows, holding bewilderment and recognition together.

<a id="an-image-could-preserve-the-relationship"></a>

## An image could preserve the relationship

A visual response might hold one ordinary detail through a radical change of scale: a river, a place, a recognisable human silhouette. The large world needs the small witness, and the witness needs something recognisable within the large world. Keeping that relationship in the composition could make scale felt through the particulars that survive it.

I would delay the hermitage rather than place it in the opening diagram. First the reader encounters the unexpected world; only then does a small familiar shape bring it close. The visual sequence can let recognition happen, instead of labelling a nested universe before the witness has found his place in it.

A [familiar rhythm](https://sleepwalker.in/blog/the-world-inside-a-breath/) and a familiar place can each give an immense frame a point of contact. The breath supplies an interval we can experience; the hermitage supplies a place the witness recognises. Neither small thing merely demonstrates how large the frame has become. Each preserves a point from which that frame can be experienced.

## The order of recognition

**Note**

**The reading follows one particular edition.** It uses the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust's English rendering of the twelfth canto of the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*.

[12.9.27](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/9/27/) supplies the inhalation and the witness's bewilderment. [12.9.28–29](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/9/28-29/) elaborates the recognised world and its temporal divisions. [12.9.30](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/9/30/) supplies the recognition of his own hermitage and the return with the exhalation.

<a id="gazette-notes-containment-and-encounter-are-different-orders"></a>

### Containment and encounter are different orders

A containment sketch could put the world inside the child and the witness inside that world. It would describe a spatial relationship but omit the order in which Markandeya encounters it. The passage supplies a succession: entry, an elaborated world, recognition of his own hermitage, then return. The listed occupations and divisions of time give that world ongoing activity, not only nested scenery.

Imagine two visual responses to that succession. The first begins with the whole arrangement visible, including the hermitage. Later panels draw closer to the witness. The reader knows where he is before he does; his recognition becomes an experience the reader watches him reach. The second begins at entry, follows rivers and inhabited places, and delays the familiar hermitage. Here the reader can share the discovery rather than wait for the witness to catch up. Both responses could retain the same spatial arrangement and finish at the same return. What changes is the relationship between the witness's knowledge and the reader's.

The second response still needs more than a caption saying “his hermitage”. A recurring contour, arrangement or object could make the place recognisable before it is named. If that recognition depends on visual information never given to the reader, the caption supplies an explanation but the image cannot supply the encounter. The compositional question is which familiar detail can survive the new scale long enough to be recognised.

| Visual form | What it makes available immediately | What it can withhold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nested containment diagram | Child, world and witness as simultaneous relations | Little of the sequence of discovery |
| Ordered panels | Entry, encounter and return as successive experiences | The hermitage until recognition becomes consequential |

Giving the reader information earlier is not necessarily a lesser choice. It can make the witness's hesitation, disbelief or eventual recognition the centre of attention. Withholding it instead puts discovery at the centre. The arrangement earns its form through the encounter it makes possible, not through a contest over which picture is more cosmically accurate.
